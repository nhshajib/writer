# Privacy request and breach-response procedures

Procedures to adopt and exercise, not proof that appointed people, contracts or controls exist. Assign an accountable owner and backup. Counsel must review jurisdiction, exceptions and retention. Do not send real incident notifications just to test this document.

## Rights requests

1. Monitor support@typewit.com, including requests without a prescribed subject. Log receipt, scope, right, owner and normally one-month deadline.
2. Verify identity proportionately using existing account/email where possible. Do not request government ID by default. Protect third-party data.
3. Inventory authentication/profile, age/guardian records, agreements/preferences, connections, source text/formatting/execution, schedules/settings/progress, logs/playback, notifications, billing/pass/trial records, support and identifiable technical logs. The Settings summary is not complete.
4. Provide Article 15 information/copies. For Article 20, assess provided data + automated processing + consent/contract basis; supply JSON/CSV/text and direct transmission where technically feasible. Do not disclose passwords, active credentials, keys or other users' information; assess and explain necessary exceptions.
5. For erasure, assess each category/exception, coordinate authorized subscription/provider cleanup and keep only justified restricted records. Handle requests even if billing blocks self-service. Explain provider documents/history and device storage TypeWit cannot erase remotely.
6. Respond without undue delay, normally within one month. Explain lawful complex/numerous-request extensions of up to two further months within the first month. Explain any lawful refusal/fee and complaint/judicial remedies; preserve proportionate proof of handling.

## Personal-data breach

1. Record report and awareness time in UTC; assign incident lead/backup and counsel. Escalate with the relevant provider.
2. Contain access safely, preserve necessary evidence and rotate affected credentials. Do not indiscriminately destroy records or put customer text/secrets in logs/fixtures.
3. Assess affected people/categories/volumes, confidentiality/integrity/availability, consequences, vulnerable users, countries, encryption/key exposure and residual risk. Document all breaches and non-notification decisions.
4. Determine competent authority; do not assume a Bangladesh controller has EU one-stop-shop eligibility. Where Article 33 requires it, notify without undue delay and where feasible within 72 hours of awareness, unless risk is unlikely. Supply nature, approximate numbers, contact, consequences and measures; explain lateness and provide phases where allowed.
5. Inform affected people without undue delay if high risk, unless a justified Article 34 exception applies; use clear practical protective steps. A status page alone is not always sufficient. If acting as processor, notify the affected controller without undue delay under the applicable law/agreement.
6. Track communications, remediation and evidence; reassess as facts emerge. Review causes, retention and controls after the incident. Exercise the procedure at a proportionate interval with synthetic scenarios.

## Retention release gate

For each category document purpose/basis, active/inactive trigger, period/criteria, provider logs/backups, legal holds, notice and deletion/anonymization method. Obtain approval/legal review before prospective migrations/housekeeping. Dry-run counts without customer text and use the established tests/deployment process. This policy update does not authorize an indiscriminate history purge.
